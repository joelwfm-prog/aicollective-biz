Playbook B · Marketing · ~9 min

A month of social posts in one afternoon.

The prompt-and-schedule workflow a Regina café uses to stay consistent — from idea to a fully queued month.

Social media dies the same way every time: you post daily for two weeks, get busy, and go quiet for a month. Customers notice the silence more than they notice any single post. The fix isn't posting more often — it's batching the whole month in one sitting so daily posting stops depending on daily willpower. Here's the workflow.

1. Pick your monthly themes

Before opening any AI tool, choose 3-5 themes you'll rotate through all month. For most small businesses that's something like: a behind-the-scenes look, a customer question answered, a product or service spotlight, a local/seasonal tie-in, and a plain "we're open, here's what's new" post. Themes matter because they give an AI tool structure — "write me social posts" produces mush, "write me posts following these five recurring themes" produces something usable.

2. Batch-generate the month with one prompt

This is the actual time-saver: one detailed prompt, one sitting, a month of drafts. Give the AI tool your themes, your voice, your platform, and how many posts you need. Be specific about tone and length or you'll get generic marketing-speak back.

You are helping a small [type of business] in [town], Saskatchewan plan a month of Instagram/Facebook posts. Our voice is [e.g. warm, a little cheeky, never corporate]. Write 20 post drafts for the next 4 weeks, rotating evenly through these 5 themes: 1) behind-the-scenes, 2) answering a customer question, 3) spotlight on one product/service, 4) local/seasonal tie-in, 5) simple update or open-hours reminder. For each post give: a short caption (2-4 sentences, no corporate buzzwords), 3-5 relevant hashtags, and a one-line note on what photo or video would pair with it. Number them Week 1 Post 1, Week 1 Post 2, etc.

Twenty posts, one prompt, roughly the length of a coffee break to generate.

3. Edit every post for voice

Do not skip this step — it's what separates "clearly AI-written" from "sounds like us." Read each draft out loud. If a line makes you wince or sounds like a chain store wrote it, rewrite it in your own words. This usually takes less time than writing from scratch, because you're editing instead of staring at a blank box.

4. Load everything into a scheduler

Once edited, drop all 20 posts into a scheduling tool (most social platforms have a built-in one, and there are inexpensive third-party options) and set them to go out on a rhythm — say, every weekday. This is the step that actually buys back your month: the posting now happens whether or not you remember to log in.

5. Repurpose across platforms

Your strongest 4-5 posts from the batch are worth a second life. Turn a caption into a 30-second video script, or stretch a "behind-the-scenes" post into a short newsletter blurb. Ask your AI tool directly: "Turn post #7 into a 30-second video script with a hook in the first 3 seconds." One good idea, three formats.

Local example: a Regina café

Say you run a café in Regina's Cathedral neighbourhood. Historically, social media happened in bursts — a flurry of posts when you remembered, then three weeks of nothing. One Sunday afternoon, using the themes and prompt above, you generated a month of drafts: new pastry spotlights, a "how we source our beans" post, a couple of answers to questions you get constantly ("are you dog-friendly?"), and two seasonal posts tied to a Regina festival. You spent 40 minutes editing for voice, loaded everything into a free scheduler, and didn't open the app again for three weeks except to reply to comments. The feed looked more consistent than it had in a year, and it cost one afternoon.

Watch out for

Don't schedule and forget entirely — still check in every few days to reply to comments and messages, since a queued post with no human behind it feels hollow fast. And don't let every post sound identical; vary sentence length and openers so the feed doesn't read like a template got run 20 times.

Do this now

Block 90 minutes this week — themes, one prompt, an editing pass, and loading the scheduler. That's the whole system, and it resets every month in less time than one good coffee break.
